A case study in one missing date

My dog's rabies shot had no expiry date.

This is the story of what a small grooming shop should do about that, and the system I built to answer it.

Chapter 1

It started with Jaddi

Jaddi gets groomed regularly, and like every dog at every grooming shop, he needs proof of a current rabies vaccination before he goes on the table.

The proof I had was an invoice from his vet. It named the rabies vaccine. It gave the date he got it. And nowhere on the page did it say when that vaccination expires.

One question, and a small business has to answer it every day: is this dog covered?

Chapter 2

The guess that becomes the record

A rabies vaccine can be good for one year or three. Both are legal. Which one Jaddi got is printed on the certificate, and the certificate is what the health department will want to see.

A grooming shop doesn't have a data team. It has a front counter, a folder of paper, and whoever is working today. So somebody looks at the invoice, makes a reasonable guess, and writes it down. From that moment on, the guess is the record. Nothing downstream will ever question it, because in a small business there is no downstream.

Chapter 3

So I asked an AI to read it

The obvious modern fix is to photograph the paperwork and let AI read it. So I tested that, field by field, against answer keys I labelled by hand.

On clean documents it was perfect. On a phone photo, which is what a busy counter actually produces, it read a smudged Jan 2?, 2027 as a crisp Jan 29, 2027. It did that about fifteen times on one page, and a better prompt didn't fix it.

The AI wasn't failing. It was doing exactly what the person at the counter does: filling the gap with a confident guess. It was just doing it faster.

Chapter 4

A system that's allowed to say "I don't know"

So the rule I built everything around is simple: no expiry date, no record. Jaddi's invoice is kept, what was read from it is kept, and no vaccination record is created. He stays "not cleared" until the shop has the real certificate.

That rule doesn't live in a training manual or in the app's screens, where a busy day could route around it. It lives in the database itself, alongside the shop's other judgment calls:

  • A dog can't start a groom while a required vaccine is expired, missing, or disputed.
  • A date the AI read becomes a record only after a person checks it.
  • A date nobody can read becomes a request to the owner for a better copy, not a guess.
  • A shave-down can't be recorded without a coat assessment that justifies it.
  • A reminder can't go to an owner who opted out.

37 rules in all, each one proven by a test that checks the database says no: 483 of them, all passing.

Chapter 5

Now you're at the counter

The groomers never see any of that. They see one screen: who's grooming today, every dog with a green check when it's cleared, its usual haircut, its allergies, the note that it hates having its back feet touched, and a calendar of what's coming up.
