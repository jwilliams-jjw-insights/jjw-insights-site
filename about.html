About JJW Insights

Analytics capacity for organizations that don't have a data team — built on a decade of federal data experience.

The Problem We Solve

We work with small agencies, nonprofits, clinics, and community organizations — teams doing essential work without a data department behind them. For them the problem usually isn't bad analytics; it's no analytics capacity at all: data scattered across spreadsheets and vendor platforms, reporting that eats a week every quarter, and nobody on staff whose job is to fix it.

JJW Insights Analytics exists to be that capacity — a partner who names the problem, builds the fix, proves it worked, and hands it over documented. No complexity theater, no dependency on us afterward.

Our Approach

Every engagement starts with the same three questions: What does your data look like now? What should it look like after we work together? And how will we know it worked?

That framing isn't marketing language — it's the operating discipline behind every project. We design governance frameworks, build interactive dashboards, and architect data pipelines that answer those three questions with evidence, not assertions. And every engagement ends the same way: with the documentation and training your team needs to keep the system working after we leave.

Background

JJW Insights Analytics LLC is led by Jason Williams, a data analytics consultant who specializes in data governance, data storytelling, and interactive visualization. With a Ph.D. in Geosciences, an M.S. in Healthcare Analytics, and over a decade of federal agency experience spanning EPA, USGS, DHS, and FEMA, Jason brings a rare combination of scientific rigor and practical delivery — from data governance frameworks and QA standards to interactive dashboards and automated data pipelines.

That federal background matters. It means rigorous QA standards, an instinct for documentation, and a respect for the weight of public data — combined with the agility and accountability that only a small, founder-led practice can offer.
